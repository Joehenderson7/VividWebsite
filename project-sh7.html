SH 7 Permanent Repairs Project

Field and laboratory materials testing for the permanent restoration of the South St. Vrain Canyon between Lyons and Raymond.

Project Overview

Rebuilding a canyon highway lost to the 2013 flood.

The September 2013 flood devastated Colorado Highway 7 through the South St. Vrain Canyon, severely damaging or completely washing away nine miles of roadway and blocking canyon access for two and a half months. The SH 7 Permanent Repairs project — the final permanent infrastructure project of Colorado's $750 million 2013 Flood Recovery Program — permanently rebuilt 14 miles of the canyon between Lyons and Raymond.

Vivid Engineering Group worked alongside Kiewit Infrastructure Co. and the Colorado Department of Transportation, providing field and laboratory materials testing of soils, aggregates, concrete, and asphalt to verify that every material placed met CDOT standards. The project replaced temporary post-flood asphalt with a fully reconstructed roadway, restored 11,500 linear feet of the St. Vrain Creek to a resilient, natural channel, rebuilt embankments and drainage, added rockfall ditches and a four-foot uphill bicycle shoulder, and revegetated 28 acres of disturbed canyon — all while achieving a net-zero construction waste goal.
