I-70 Floyd Hill to Chief Hosa Resurfacing

Owner acceptance testing and inspection through 6.4 miles of night paving on Colorado's mountain corridor.

Project Overview

Repaving the gateway to the Rockies — without closing it.

Between Floyd Hill and Chief Hosa, I-70 climbs through the front range foothills carrying ski traffic, freight, and daily commuters in and out of the mountains — and its pavement had reached the end of its life. CDOT's $12.8 million resurfacing project rehabilitated 6.4 miles of the interstate (MP 246.5–252.9) through Jefferson and Clear Creek counties, milling off deteriorated asphalt and repaving both directions while replacing guardrail, cleaning culverts, and upgrading signage and striping. With no practical detour around this stretch of the mountain corridor, contractor APC Construction ran the milling and paving trains almost entirely under nighttime lane closures across two paving seasons.

Working directly for CDOT, Vivid Engineering Group provided construction inspection, QA/QC administration, and field and laboratory owner acceptance materials testing across the project. Vivid inspectors and certified technicians worked the night closures alongside the paving crews — verifying mix temperatures, compaction, and placement in real time — while samples of asphalt, aggregates, and soils ran through our AASHTO-accredited Colorado laboratories. High-altitude paving windows, mountain weather, and live interstate traffic made every shift count; Vivid's team kept acceptance testing and documentation moving on the contractor's schedule so the corridor reopened to full capacity every morning.
