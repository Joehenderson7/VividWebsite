Colorado's Power Pathway

Owner's representative quality assurance and geotechnical engineering across all five segments of Xcel Energy's 550-mile transmission expansion.

Project Overview

The backbone of Colorado's clean-energy future.

Colorado's Power Pathway is Xcel Energy's $1.7 billion expansion of the state's high-voltage grid — approximately 550 miles of new double-circuit 345 kV transmission line looping through twelve counties of eastern Colorado, along with new and expanded substations, to deliver roughly 5,500 megawatts of new wind and solar generation planned through 2030. Vivid Engineering Group has served as owner's representative on all five segments of the program, providing quality assurance testing and inspection on transmission structure foundations and substation sites from the Fort St. Vrain generating station to the Harvest Mile substation.

On site since 2023, our field teams and geotechnical engineers have verified the integrity of the drilled foundations that carry every steel structure along the route, and supported construction at the program's new substations — Canal Crossing, Goose Creek, and May Valley — as well as expansions of existing facilities. Working across the open plains of eastern Colorado, often far from the nearest lab, Vivid's crews have kept sampling, testing, and inspection moving at the pace of one of the largest transmission builds in the western United States.
